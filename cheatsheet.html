Local meeting transcription · nothing leaves this Mac

mt — start a recording in one line

Works for any meeting — Zoom, Teams, Meet, Dialpad, a phone on speaker, or a conversation in a room. It records what the Mac plays and what your mic hears, so it does not care which app the call is in.

mt live --customer acme --name acme-weekly --title "Acme // Weekly"

Yes — combine --customer and --name. They do different jobs: the first picks the folder, the second names the meeting inside it. Drop --name and you get a generic 2026-09-04-meeting, which gets confusing by the third one.

How a meeting runs

  1. 1

    Start it in its own tab

    A separate terminal tab, so Ctrl+C is clean and does not interrupt anything else.
  2. 2

    Have the meeting

    A rough transcript prints as you go. Switch apps and move windows freely — it reads the meeting window even when it is buried.
  3. 3

    Press Ctrl+C

    That ends the recording and automatically starts the accurate pass. Roughly 5 minutes for an hour-long meeting.
  4. 4

    Name anyone it could not

    Once per person, ever. After that they are recognised automatically in every future meeting.
mt enroll <session-dir> --speaker 2 --name "Alex Example"

The five commands worth remembering

mt live
The one you want. Rolling transcript during the call, accurate pass after.
mt record
Same, without the live text. Slightly less to think about.
mt process <file>
Transcribe a recording you already have — a Zoom download, a Gong mp3, a voice memo.
mt speakers
Who is enrolled so far.
mt doctor
Run this first if anything misbehaves. It names the fix.
Keep the pop-out visible

Zoom's floating window and Meet's pop-out both display who is currently talking, and that is where the speaker names come from. Leaving one up is the single best thing you can do for attribution.

Customer slugs for --customer

Files the meeting into your workspace: ~/mt-data/Customers/<slug>/meetings/YYYY-MM-DD-name/

acme globex

The slug is the folder name under Customers/. Leave --customer off entirely for internal calls like CAB and it lands in Recordings/.

Flags you will actually reach for

--title "CAB — Sep 4"
The heading inside the transcript. Spaces are fine here.
--speakers 6
When you know how many people are on the far end. Sharpens speaker splitting.
--roster "Alex,Sam"
Names you expect, if you have them. Treated as a guess, never as fact.
--in-person
Several people sharing your mic in a room. Splits your own track too. Forgot it? mt process <session> --in-person --no-system re-splits afterwards.
--no-system
Mic only, in person. The system track is still recorded, but its watchdog is off, so no spoken "System audio check" in the room.
--no-mic
A webinar or playback where you never speak. On mt process: ignore the mic track and use only the system (Teams) track.
--duration 3600
Stop on its own after N seconds, in case you forget.

Which mic is it using?

macOS quietly changes the default input when you move between home and the office — plug in a dock or a webcam and it can grab the input slot without telling you. So mt names the mic it picked before it starts, and records it in the transcript header and capture.json afterwards.

mt devices
▸ in the list
The current system default input.
"mt would use:"
What you will actually get, and the reason it was chosen.
--mic-id HyperX
Override for one meeting. A fragment of the name is enough — you never need the long CoreAudio id.
mic_priority
In config.json. Ordered list of name fragments; the first one plugged in wins, so both locations just work.
Webcam and dock mics are the trap

They sound the worst and are the most likely to seize the default slot the moment you connect them. Both are deliberately left out of mic_priority, so a headset or the HyperX wins whenever one is present — and the built-in MacBook mic is the last resort rather than an accident.

What you get, and what to keep

  • transcript.mdThe record. Speaker-labelled and timestamped, with a table of who was seen on screen.
  • transcript.txtFlat text. Good for pasting to your AI assistant.
  • live.mdThe rough live notes. Superseded by transcript.md.
  • speakers.jsonEdit a name here, then run mt relabel <dir>.
  • system.flac / mic.flacThe audio, losslessly compressed. A 33-minute call keeps about 56 MB instead of 764 MB.
  • screen_roster.jsonWhat was read off the meeting window, with frame counts.
  • work/Intermediates. Safe to delete; only makes a re-run slower.
Then hand it to your AI assistant

“Summarise Customers/acme/meetings/2026-09-04-acme-weekly/transcript.md and pull out action items.” The transcript is the raw material; the summary is your AI assistant’s job.

What it can and can't tell you

“Faster and higher than her normal” — yes. Why — no: excited and upset sound the same. The words describe delivery, never feelings or honesty. It stays in mt web; it never goes into a summary, weekly, Jira or Slack. Every panel has a Field guide.

If something looks wrong

What you seeWhy, and what to do
Your own lines are missing You were too quiet, or the HyperX mute plate is tapped off. Gain is capped on purpose — below about −60 dBFS a track is treated as noise and dropped rather than invented over.
A garbled copy of the far side, under your name Your mic is hearing the call through the speakers. Wear headphones. It is filtered out, but headphones remove it at the source.
Far too many speakers Pass --speakers N. Long calls split one person across several voices; that number pins it.
Names spelled wrong Add them to prompt in config.json. It holds about 150 words — swap terms in and out rather than appending forever.
The first few seconds are missing The system-audio tap occasionally takes a moment to attach. Start it a few seconds before the meeting.
Nothing captured at all Run mt doctor. Almost always Screen & System Audio Recording being off for the terminal, which needs a full ⌘+Q after you grant it.
Speaker names are always marked unconfirmed

Every machine-derived name — voiceprint match, on-screen label, roster order — is written as a guess, and the transcript says so. Confirm before quoting anyone in a customer-facing artifact. A 0.92 voiceprint match is strong evidence, not proof.
